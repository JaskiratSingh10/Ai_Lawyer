## 1. Executive Summary

This report establishes the traceable provenance, statutory validity, commencement timeline, amendment status, and enforcement reality of all legislative instruments included in the **Indian Legislation Corpus for Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)**.

A central requirement of legal-data curation under Indian jurisprudence is distinguishing between **legislative enactment** (passage by Parliament or State Assemblies and Presidential/Gubernatorial assent) and **statutory commencement** (enforcement via official Gazette notification under an appointed-date clause). In accordance with the curator brief:
- Dates, versions, and legal statuses are verified directly against primary official government sources: **India Code**, the **eGazette of India**, Ministry portals (**Ministry of Consumer Affairs**, **Ministry of Labour and Employment**, **Ministry of Law and Justice**), and State Government gazettes.
- No search snippets, unofficial aggregators, or AI-generated assumptions are treated as authoritative law.
- Where an enacted provision has not yet been notified for commencement, it is explicitly flagged as **`Substantively Not in Force / Pending Central Notification`**, with its operational implications analyzed alongside the surviving legacy statutes.

### Domain 1: Consumer Protection

#### 1. The Consumer Protection Act, 2019 (Act No. 35 of 2019)
- **Jurisdiction**: Central (Union of India).
- **Act Type**: Central Act.
- **Enactment Date**: 9 August 2019 (Presidential assent).
- **Commencement / Effective Dates**:
  - *Phase 1 (General Provisions & Commissions)*: Sections 2 to 19, 28 to 87, 90, 91, 94, 95, 99 to 107 were brought into force on **20 July 2020** vide Ministry of Consumer Affairs Notification **S.O. 2351(E)** dated 15 July 2020.
  - *Phase 2 (Central Consumer Protection Authority - CCPA)*: Sections 10, 20, 21, 22, 23, 24, 25, 26, 27 were brought into force on **24 July 2020** vide Notification **S.O. 2421(E)** dated 23 July 2020.
- **Current Legal Status**: **In Force**.
- **Repeal & Replacement Status**: Section 107 expressly repealed the *Consumer Protection Act, 1986 (68 of 1986)*. Ongoing proceedings instituted under the 1986 Act continue to be governed as provided by Section 107(2) read with Section 6 of the General Clauses Act, 1897.
- **Pecuniary Jurisdiction Revision**: While Sections 34(1), 47(1)(a)(i), and 58(1)(a)(i) originally set pecuniary ceilings at ₹1 Crore (District), ₹10 Crores (State), and > ₹10 Crores (National), the statutory proviso to each section empowered the Central Government to prescribe alternate values. This was executed vide **G.S.R. 912(E)** on 30 December 2021 (see below).
- **Primary Source URL**: `https://www.indiacode.nic.in/handle/123456789/15256`

#### 2. The Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules, 2020
- **Jurisdiction**: Central (Union of India).
- **Act / Rule Type**: Central Subordinate Legislation (Rules).
- **Statutory Authority**: Section 101(1)(zg) read with Section 94 of the Consumer Protection Act, 2019.
- **Notification**: **G.S.R. 462(E)**, published in the Gazette of India Extraordinary, Part II, Section 3, Sub-section (i), dated **23 July 2020**.
- **Commencement Date**: **23 July 2020** (came into force on the date of publication in the Official Gazette).
- **Current Legal Status**: **In Force**.
- **Key Regulatory Provisions**: Applies to all digital commerce (inventory and marketplace models) and extra-territorially to overseas entities targeting Indian consumers (Rule 2(2)). Mandates grievance officer acknowledgment within 48 hours and redressal within 1 month (Rule 4(1)(d)), bans pre-ticked consent boxes (Rule 4(1)(h)), prohibits fake reviews (Rule 6(2)), and requires mandatory refunds for defective or late goods (Rules 6(3) & 7(4)).
- **Primary Source URL**: `https://consumeraffairs.gov.in/acts-and-rules/consumer-protection`

#### 3. Consumer Protection (Jurisdiction of the District Commission, the State Commission and the National Commission) Rules, 2021
- **Jurisdiction**: Central (Union of India).
- **Act / Rule Type**: Central Subordinate Legislation (Rules).
- **Statutory Authority**: Proviso to Section 34(1), proviso to Section 47(1)(a)(i), and proviso to Section 58(1)(a)(i) read with Section 101(2) of the Consumer Protection Act, 2019.
- **Notification**: **G.S.R. 912(E)**, dated **30 December 2021**.
- **Commencement Date**: **30 December 2021** (date of publication).
- **Current Legal Status**: **In Force**.
- **Operative Ceilings Prescribed**:
  - **District Commission (Rule 3)**: Value of goods or services paid as consideration **does not exceed ₹50 Lakh** (reduced from ₹1 Crore).
  - **State Commission (Rule 4)**: Value of goods or services paid as consideration **exceeds ₹50 Lakh but does not exceed ₹2 Crore** (revised from ₹1 Cr – ₹10 Cr).
  - **National Commission (Rule 5)**: Value of goods or services paid as consideration **exceeds ₹2 Crore** (reduced from exceeding ₹10 Crore).
- **Primary Source URL**: `https://consumeraffairs.gov.in/acts-and-rules/consumer-protection`

#### 4. Consumer Protection (Consumer Disputes Redressal Commissions) Rules, 2020
- **Jurisdiction**: Central (Union of India).
- **Notification**: **G.S.R. 449(E)**, dated 15 July 2020, w.e.f. **20 July 2020**.
- **Current Legal Status**: **In Force**.
- **Operative Content**: Rule 7 prescribes the statutory filing fee schedule: complaints up to ₹5 Lakh have **zero fee (Nil)**; complaints ₹5 Lakh to ₹10 Lakh carry ₹200 fee, scaling up to ₹7,500 for claims exceeding ₹10 Crore before the National Commission.
- **Primary Source URL**: `https://consumeraffairs.gov.in/acts-and-rules/consumer-protection`

### Domain 2: The Limitation Act, 1963

#### 1. The Limitation Act, 1963 (Act No. 36 of 1963)
- **Jurisdiction**: Central (Union of India).
- **Act Type**: Central Act.
- **Enactment Date**: 5 October 1963.
- **Commencement Date**: **1 January 1964** vide Notification No. **S.O. 3118** dated 29 October 1963.
- **Current Legal Status**: **In Force**.
- **Key Amendments**:
  - *Jammu and Kashmir Reorganisation Act, 2019 (Act 34 of 2019)*: Extended the Act to the Union Territory of Jammu & Kashmir and the Union Territory of Ladakh w.e.f. 31 October 2019, repealing the former J&K Limitation Act, Samvat 1995.
- **Critical Jurisprudential Principles for RAG**:
  - **Section 3**: Absolute statutory bar. If a suit, appeal, or application is filed after the prescribed period, the court is legally mandated to dismiss it, even if the opposite party does not raise limitation as a defence.
  - **Section 5 vs. Original Suits**: Section 5 allows condonation of delay upon showing "sufficient cause", but it applies **strictly to appeals and applications**. It **does not apply to original suits**. Delay in filing a suit cannot be condoned under Section 5.
  - **Section 18 & 19 (Clock Reset)**: A signed written acknowledgment of liability (s. 18) or a documented part-payment (s. 19) made *before* the expiry of the limitation period creates a fresh period of limitation from the date of signature/payment.
  - **Section 29(2) (Special Laws Savings)**: Where a special or local law (such as Section 69 of the Consumer Protection Act 2019 or State Rent Acts) prescribes a specific limitation period, that period overrides the Schedule to the Limitation Act.
- **Schedule Articles Curated for Everyday Claims**:
  - **Article 7**: Unpaid wages/salary — **3 years** from when wages accrue due.
  - **Article 22**: Money deposited payable on demand (security deposits) — **3 years** from when demand is made.
  - **Article 24**: Money received for plaintiff's use (excess deductions/refunds) — **3 years** from receipt.
  - **Article 47**: Failure of consideration (refund on cancelled contract/service) — **3 years** from date of failure.
  - **Article 52**: Arrears of rent — **3 years** from when arrears become due (older arrears are irrecoverable).
  - **Article 55**: Breach of contract (general compensatory remedy) — **3 years** from breach.
  - **Article 67**: Landlord suit to recover possession from tenant — **12 years** from determination of tenancy.
  - **Article 113**: Omnibus residuary civil suit — **3 years** from when the right to sue accrues.
  - **Article 137**: Omnibus residuary application — **3 years** from when the right to apply accrues.
- **Primary Source URL**: `https://www.indiacode.nic.in/handle/123456789/1566`

### Domain 3: Wage and Employment Laws (Labour Codes & Preserved Legacy Enactments)

#### 1. The Code on Wages, 2019 (Act No. 29 of 2019)
- **Jurisdiction**: Central (Union of India).
- **Act Type**: Central Act.
- **Enactment Date**: 8 August 2019 (received Presidential assent).
- **Commencement Status**: **IN FORCE** w.e.f. **21 November 2025**.
  - *Official Gazette Notification*: Brought fully into force across India on **21 November 2025** vide Ministry of Labour and Employment Notification **S.O. 5322(E)**, published in the Gazette of India Extraordinary.
  - *Effective From*: `2025-11-21` | *Repealed On*: `None`.
- **Repeal & Savings Reality (Section 67)**: Section 67 formally repealed the *Payment of Wages Act, 1936*, the *Minimum Wages Act, 1948*, the *Payment of Bonus Act, 1965*, and the *Equal Remuneration Act, 1976* w.e.f. 21 November 2025. Under Section 67(2) read with Section 6 of the *General Clauses Act, 1897*, previous operations, rights, privileges, obligations, or liabilities acquired or incurred under the repealed enactments remain actionable for pre-21-Nov-2025 disputes.
- **Substantive Scoped Provisions in the Code**:
  - **Section 2(y)**: Tripartite definition of wages with a statutory 50% cap limiting excluded allowances.
  - **Section 14**: Overtime wages fixed at not less than twice the normal wage rate.
  - **Section 17(2)**: Final settlement mandate requiring all wages to be paid **within two working days** of resignation, dismissal, retrenchment, or closure.
  - **Section 18**: Exhaustive statutory list of lawful deductions; total deductions capped at 50% of wages.
  - **Section 45**: Wage claims filed before the Authority within 3 years, with compensatory power up to ten times the claim amount.
- **Primary Source URL**: `https://labour.gov.in/sites/default/files/the_code_on_wages_2019_no._29_of_2019.pdf`

#### 2. The Code on Social Security, 2020 (Act No. 36 of 2020)
- **Jurisdiction**: Central (Union of India).
- **Act Type**: Central Act.
- **Enactment Date**: 28 September 2020.
- **Commencement Status**: **IN FORCE** w.e.f. **21 November 2025**.
  - *Official Gazette Notification*: Brought into force on **21 November 2025** vide Ministry of Labour and Employment Notification **S.O. 5323(E)**.
  - *Effective From*: `2025-11-21` | *Repealed On*: `None`.
- **Repeal & Savings Reality (Section 164)**: Section 164 formally repealed the *Payment of Gratuity Act, 1972* and the *Employees' Provident Funds and Miscellaneous Provisions Act, 1952* w.e.f. 21 November 2025. Under Section 164(2) and Section 6 of the General Clauses Act, 1897, gratuity or PF liabilities incurred prior to 21-Nov-2025 are governed by the repealed legacy Acts.
- **Substantive Scoped Provisions in the Code**:
  - **Section 17**: EPF contribution of 10% or 12% by employer and employee.
  - **Section 53**: Gratuity payable on termination/resignation after 5 years continuous service ($15/26 \times \text{last drawn wages}$). Crucially, the 5-year barrier is eliminated for **fixed-term employment** (pro-rata entitlement).
  - **Section 55**: Employer must determine gratuity and pay within 30 days; delayed payment incurs mandatory simple interest.
- **Primary Source URL**: `https://labour.gov.in/sites/default/files/SS_Code_Gazette.pdf`

#### 3. The Industrial Relations Code, 2020 (Act No. 35 of 2020)
- **Jurisdiction**: Central (Union of India).
- **Act Type**: Central Act.
- **Enactment Date**: 28 September 2020.
- **Commencement Status**: **IN FORCE** w.e.f. **21 November 2025**.
  - *Official Gazette Notification*: Brought into force on **21 November 2025** vide Ministry of Labour and Employment Notification **S.O. 5320(E)**.
  - *Effective From*: `2025-11-21` | *Repealed On*: `None`.
- **Repeal & Savings Reality (Section 104)**: Section 104 formally repealed the *Industrial Disputes Act, 1947* w.e.f. 21 November 2025, preserving pending claims and liabilities.
- **Substantive Scoped Provisions in the Code**:
  - **Section 70**: Conditions precedent to retrenchment in establishments with < 300 workers: 1 month notice or pay in lieu, 15 days average pay per completed year of service, and notice to government.

  - **Section 83**: Mandatory contribution by employer of 15 days last drawn wages into the Worker Reskilling Fund within 45 days of retrenchment.
- **Primary Source URL**: `https://labour.gov.in/sites/default/files/IR_Gazette_of_India.pdf`

#### 4. Preserved Fallback Legacy Acts (Enforceable for Pre-21-Nov-2025 Claims)
Because disputes frequently concern wages, settlements, gratuity, or retrenchment occurring prior to 21 November 2025, this corpus curates full standalone parent documents and exact statutory text for the legacy Acts with `repealed_on: "2025-11-21"`:
- **Payment of Wages Act, 1936 (`IND-FED-PWA-1936`)**:
  - *Effective From*: `1937-03-28` | *Repealed On*: `2025-11-21`.
  - *Section 5(2)*: Requires earned wages upon termination of employment to be paid before the expiry of the **second working day**.
  - *Section 15*: Filing claims for unpaid wages or unauthorized deductions within 12 months before the Authority, with compensation up to 10 times deducted wages.
- **Payment of Gratuity Act, 1972 (`IND-FED-PGA-1972`)**:
  - *Effective From*: `1972-09-16` | *Repealed On*: `2025-11-21`.
  - *Section 4*: Gratuity eligibility after 5 years continuous service calculated at 15 days wages per year based on $15/26 \times \text{last drawn basic+DA}$, capped at ₹20 Lakhs.
  - *Section 7*: Mandatory payment within 30 days; statutory simple interest under Section 7(3A) for unjustified delay.
- **Minimum Wages Act, 1948 (`IND-FED-MWA-1948`)**:
  - *Effective From*: `1948-03-15` | *Repealed On*: `2025-11-21`.
  - *Section 12 & 14*: Mandatory payment of notified minimum rates and overtime at double normal rates for work performed prior to 21 November 2025.
- **Employees' Provident Funds Act, 1952 (`IND-FED-EPF-1952`)**:
  - *Effective From*: `1952-11-01` | *Repealed On*: `2025-11-21`.
  - *Section 6 & 14B*: Statutory contributions (10%/12%) and damages for delayed deposit.
- **Industrial Disputes Act, 1947 (`IND-FED-IDA-1947`)**:
  - *Effective From*: `1947-04-01` | *Repealed On*: `2025-11-21`.
  - *Section 25F*: Mandatory conditions for retrenchment of workmen (1 month notice and 15 days average pay per year).
  - *Section 33C*: Summary recovery of money due from employer before the Labour Court.

### Domain 4: Rent Laws (Central Fallback & State/UT Coverage)

#### 1. Central Fallback: Transfer of Property Act, 1882 (ss. 105 - 117)
- **Jurisdiction**: Central (Union of India).
- **Enactment Date**: 17 February 1882; **Commenced**: 1 July 1882.
- **Current Legal Status**: **In Force**.
- **Role in Corpus**: Chapter V operates as the central fallback governing tenancies in Delhi, Maharashtra, Karnataka, and other territories whenever a tenancy falls outside the scope of local rent control (e.g. rent exceeding statutory ceilings or corporate leases).
- **Key Sections**:
  - **s. 105**: Definition of lease, premium, and rent.
  - **s. 106**: Periodic tenancy notice: **15 days written notice** to terminate month-to-month tenancies, running from the date of receipt.
  - **s. 107**: Requirement of registered instrument for leases exceeding 1 year (the root of India's 11-month lease practice).
  - **s. 108**: Statutory rights and covenants: quiet enjoyment, deduction of repair costs from rent, and obligation to restore premises with reasonable wear and tear excepted.
  - **s. 111**: Determination of lease by efflux of time, forfeiture, surrender, or notice to quit.
  - **s. 114**: Equitable relief against forfeiture for non-payment of rent upon tender of arrears.
  - **s. 116**: Holding over: acceptance of rent after lease expiration creates a month-to-month renewal by operation of law.
- **Primary Source URL**: `https://www.indiacode.nic.in/handle/123456789/2338`

#### 2. Delhi: The Delhi Rent Control Act, 1958 (Act No. 59 of 1958)
- **Jurisdiction**: National Capital Territory of Delhi.
- **Enactment Date**: 31 December 1958; **Commenced**: 9 February 1959.
- **Current Legal Status**: **In Force**.
- **Critical Threshold**: Under **Section 3(c)** (inserted by the Delhi Rent Control (Amendment) Act, 1988), the Act **does not apply to any premises whose monthly rent exceeds ₹3,500**.
- **RAG Practical Impact**: Because prevailing market rents in Delhi exceed ₹3,500/month, the overwhelming majority of modern tenancies in Delhi are governed by the **Transfer of Property Act, 1882** before regular civil courts, rather than the Rent Controller.
- **Primary Source URL**: `https://www.indiacode.nic.in/handle/123456789/1531`

#### 3. Maharashtra: The Maharashtra Rent Control Act, 1999 (Maharashtra Act No. XVIII of 2000)
- **Jurisdiction**: State of Maharashtra.
- **Assented to by President**: 10 March 2000; **Commenced**: 31 March 2000.
- **Current Legal Status**: **In Force**.
- **Repealed**: *Bombay Rents, Hotel and Lodging House Rates Control Act, 1947*.
- **Key Statutory Provisions**:
  - **Section 3(1)(b)**: Exemption for premises let to banks, PSUs, multinational corporations, and private/public companies having a paid-up share capital of **₹1 Crore or more**. Such tenancies fall under TPA 1882.
  - **Section 24**: Fast-track summary eviction of licensees upon expiry of Leave and License before the Competent Authority. Licensee overstaying is liable to pay **double the license fee** as damages.
  - **Section 55**: Mandates that every tenancy / leave and license agreement **must be in writing and compulsorily registered**. The legal responsibility is on the landlord; failure makes the tenant's terms prima facie accepted by courts and constitutes a criminal offence punishable by up to 3 months imprisonment or ₹5,000 fine.
- **Primary Source URL**: `https://www.indiacode.nic.in/handle/123456789/2464`

#### 4. Karnataka: The Karnataka Rent Act, 1999 (Karnataka Act No. 34 of 2001)
- **Jurisdiction**: State of Karnataka.
- **Assented to by President**: 22 November 2001; **Commenced**: 31 December 2001 vide Notification No. RD 39 LRE 2001.
- **Current Legal Status**: **In Force**.
- **Repealed**: *Karnataka Rent Control Act, 1961 (Act 22 of 1961)*.
- **Critical Threshold**: Under **Section 2(3)(e)**, the Act does not apply to premises whose deemed or standard monthly rent exceeds **₹3,500 in Part A Schedule I areas (including Bengaluru)** or **₹2,000 elsewhere**, or commercial premises with plinth area > 14 sq meters. Exempt premises fall back to TPA 1882.
- **Primary Source URL**: `http://dpal.karnataka.gov.in`

#### 5. Tamil Nadu: The Tamil Nadu Regulation of Rights and Responsibilities of Landlords and Tenants Act, 2017 (TN Act No. 42 of 2017)
- **Jurisdiction**: State of Tamil Nadu.
- **Assented to by President**: 4 August 2017; **Commenced**: 22 February 2019 vide **G.O. Ms. No. 35**, Housing and Urban Development.
- **Current Legal Status**: **In Force**.
- **Repealed**: Section 47 expressly repealed the old *Tamil Nadu Buildings (Lease and Rent Control) Act, 1960*.
- **Key Statutory Provisions**:
  - **Section 4**: Mandatory registration of written tenancy agreement on the official Tenancy Portal (`tenancy.tn.gov.in`) with the Rent Authority within 90 days.
  - **Section 9**: Landlord must serve at least 3 months written notice prior to rent revision.
  - **Section 11**: Strict statutory cap on security deposits: **maximum 3 months' rent for residential premises** and **1 month's rent for non-residential premises**. Advance must be refunded within 1 month of vacating.
  - **Section 22**: Severe penalty for holding over: tenant failing to vacate after expiry/termination must pay **double monthly rent for the first 2 months**, and **four times monthly rent thereafter**.
- **Primary Source URL**: `https://tenancy.tn.gov.in`
